Reporting prototypes

Exploration behind the race wall design direction, in the order the thinking happened. Judged against the visualization rubric.

Only 5, 6 and 7 bear on the race review surface. 1 through 4 are season-surface forms, archived here as exploration — the season landing is settled and is not being iterated from this work.

Every roster and result on this page is synthetic. Names are two-letter placeholders and no value traces to a real athlete. Season totals were fitted to the shape of a plausible season (32 riders, 96 starts, 25/16/19/21/15 per race) so the forms could be judged at realistic density. These are drawings, not data. Safe to commit; not evidence of anything.

1 · Season participation wallexperiment

Riders as columns, races as rows, grouped by category. Columns sorted by first-start race then by starts, so full-season riders anchor the left of each block and late arrivals stack right. Group width carries composition for free. One bit per cell — solid is a finish, hollow is a start without published finish, a faint dot is no result. The orange notch marks a first recorded start after race 1. Row totals on the right make a separate starts/finishes bar chart redundant.

2 · Participation depthexperiment

Every rider still one mark, but the axis is commitment rather than calendar. Answers whether the season is a few regulars plus a long tail or a broad base. The U shape is the finding. Cost: loses which races.

3 · Roster of pulsesexperiment

Small multiples — each Descender an object with a name attached rather than a coordinate. The version a coach can point at, and the one that survives being filtered to a squad or printed. Cost: real estate, and 32 glyphs is near the upper bound before it reads as confetti.

4 · Arrival cohortsexperiment

Same 96 starts, reframed as recruitment and retention. Each rider a unit block shaded by the race they first started. Reframes a mid-season dip as "who didn't come back" rather than "attendance was low." Cost: riders aren't traceable across columns — cohorts, not individuals.

5 · Race board on a shared axissuperseded

First attempt at the race surface: all club riders on one axis, percent behind the category winner, full field in grey. The grey-field idea survives into the direction. The anchor does not survive as drawn, because percent back off total time is incoherent once the bonus lap exists. Superseded by 7.

6 · Why total time failsreference

Explanatory, not a candidate surface. Above: total time splits a category into two lap cohorts with two separate zeros and no scale spanning them. Below: comparing over the base distance every rider completed restores one axis and puts the cutoff on it. Note the hollow mark left of the cutoff — a rider with base laps recorded whose split was inside the line. We do not know why, and nothing on the chart may guess.

7 · Bonus-lap distributionsuperseded

Superseded on 2026-09-10 by the winner-anchored field strip in race-wall.md §2. Its region and marker vocabulary still hold. As first proposed: zero is the bonus-lap cutoff, not the winner. Unit is clock time. Left of the line is a success region and the tint does that work — no badge. The shaded band right of the line is labeled by its measured width, never as "nearly made it." Full field in grey answers the question a coach actually has. Shown here with three categories; the built version carries every category, and the cutoff renders as a band wherever it is derived rather than published.
